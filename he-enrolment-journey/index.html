Introduction

Every HE student ends up in the same place — a confirmed set of Subject Offerings tied to Academic Periods, financial liability created, TCSI records prepared. But two very different journeys can get them there: an administrator can drive the whole process from Ready Student Core, or the student can drive it themselves from the Plus Student Portal.

Under the surface, both journeys share the same backend services, the same requisite engine, and the same six validation gates. What differs is who is allowed to override what, and how much has to be in place before the front door even opens.

🕐

Less admin effort

Routine subject enrolments move to the student — freeing staff for the exceptions that genuinely need them.

🎓

Student autonomy

Students pick their electives and shape their study plan themselves, from any device, whenever the enrolment window is open.

🛡️

Rules always enforced

Requisites, program structure and enrolment windows are checked live. Unlike admins, students can never override them.

🔀

Two paths, one engine

Admin and self-enrol go through the same validation engine. One source of truth, whether staff or students drive the wizard.

1Setup — before either wizard can open

Everything the student — or their administrator — experiences is controlled upstream on Ready Student Core and Curriculum Builder. Three layers matter: the curriculum has to exist, the course has to be structured, and (for self-enrol only) the right switches have to be flipped.

a. Curriculum Builder — the source of truth

Curriculum Builder (CB) is a separate app reached through Core at Administration → Curriculum → Curriculum Builder. HE Programs, Subjects, versions and requisites all live here. Setting a Program or Subject Version to Active is what triggers the one-way sync from CB to Core. Only Active items sync.

🗝️
CB is where HE lives. RS Core's engine is VET-focused and can't natively express HE structures — programs, subjects, versioning, requisites, structured groups. CB fills that gap and hands the results to Core automatically.

b. RS Core — calendars, offerings, course, student

Once CB has synced, everything else is built in Core: Academic Periods with Period Types (one must be marked Primary), Subject Offerings that tie a synced subject version to a period + location + attendance mode, the HE Course itself, and the student enrolment against the course. Any period the program doesn't include is invisible to the wizard.

c. Feature flags & provider settings (self-enrol only)

The admin path skips this section entirely — Manage Enrolment appears the moment an HE enrolment exists. Self-enrol needs the full stack of flags in place, plus a "yearly" enrolment pattern for year-scope and non-null self-enrolment dates on the primary Academic Period.

🚩

Portal flag chain

Four portal switches, from the base Student Portal up to Higher Ed and finally HE Self-Enrolment. All four must be on.

⚙️

Dev flags

Curriculum Engine and Curriculum Builder under Development feature flags — superuser only. Powers requisite & program-structure validation.

🏢

Provider setting

HE Self-Enrolment is also a provider-level preference, separate from the feature flags. If it is off, the wizard is unreachable.

📅

Pattern & dates

Subject Enrolment Pattern = Yearly unlocks year-scope. Primary AP needs non-null open & close dates.

💡
The setup story in one line: curriculum in CB, calendar in Core, and (if self-enrol) flags and dates. Miss any one and the wizard stays closed.

2Login & entry points

Two front doors, two different actors — but both lead to the same wizard engine. The administrator signs into RS Core; the student signs into the Plus Student Portal. Each surface only shows the enrolment action when the platform confirms it's allowed.

🔐
Credentials, one place. Student logins are provisioned in RS Core the moment a student record is created. Admins log in with their staff credentials — the same sign-in service that also authenticates Curriculum Builder.

3The fork — where the two journeys diverge

Same enrolment, same offerings, same engine. From here the journey shape depends entirely on who is driving.

Admin path

Administrator drives

Staff at the institution — admissions or student services — running the enrolment inside Ready Student Core.

  • Opens Manage Enrolment on the student record
  • Sees offerings without portal-flag gating
  • Can override Requisites & Standard Load with a reason
  • Handles exceptions (backdated enrolments, waivers)
— fork —
Student path

Student drives

The student themselves, from the Plus Student Portal, inside the guardrails their institution set up in advance.

  • Sees the Enrol action when the window is open
  • One of three wizard scopes: Year, Period, or Adjust
  • Cannot override any validation — hard gates for all
  • Guided, mobile-friendly, saveable mid-flow

4The admin-led journey

Five steps from Manage Enrolment to a confirmed enrolment. The wizard runs inside RS Core; the admin can override two gates on the student's behalf.

Manage Enrolment→ Enrol Student→ Select offerings→ Review & validate→ Confirm

  1. Open Manage Enrolment

    From the student's Enrolment tab in RS Core. HE-only surface — VET students don't see it.

  2. Click Enrol Student against a period

    Each academic period on the enrolment shows an Enrol action. Clicking opens the wizard scoped to that period.

  3. Select offerings

    Pick the Subject Offerings for the period. The pool matches what a self-enrolling student would see — but the admin isn't gated by portal flags.

  4. Review & validate

    The system runs the six validation checks (§6). Two — Requisites and Standard Load — can be overridden here with a captured reason.

  5. Confirm

    Multi-period confirms are atomic — a failure in one AP rolls back all of them. On success, financial liability is created and TCSI records are updated.

🔑
What the admin has that the student doesn't: override authority on Requisites and Standard Load. Every other gate — Program rules, Offering status, Calendar constraints, Duplicate prevention — is hard for both paths.

5The student self-enrolment journey

A guided wizard the student drives themselves, in one of three scopes chosen by URL — not a dropdown. Each scope has its own eligibility rules; if they aren't met, that wizard isn't available.

Login→ Dashboard / Enrolments→ Enrol→ Wizard→ Add subjects→ Review→ Confirm

a. Three scopes, one wizard

Which wizard the student gets depends on the institution's Subject Enrolment Pattern and what they're doing (fresh enrolment vs. modifying an existing one).

📆

Year-scope

Multi-period across a calendar year. Requires the Yearly enrolment pattern and a Primary academic period whose close date is still ahead.

📅

Period-scope

A single academic period. The period must be Primary, with both self-enrolment dates set.

🔄

Adjust-scope

Modify an existing enrolment — add or drop. Available while either the close date or the drop deadline is still ahead.

b. Choosing subjects — the drawer

Clicking Add subjects opens a side drawer with every offering available to this student, in this period, in their program. Search as you type, and filter by Core or Elective. Search and filter survive a page reload.

c. Review page

A read-only summary of the student's draft picks, grouped by academic period. Per-period StudyLoadAlert shows what percentage of the standard load the draft represents. No mutation controls — just Back, Confirm, and Save & exit.

⚠️
Multi-AP atomicity. A single failure in any academic period rolls back the entire confirm. If a student had 3 subjects across 2 APs and one requisite check fails, none of the enrolments land. Design test cases with that in mind.

d. Save & resume

Save & exit saves the draft to the server, so the student can pick up exactly where they left off, from any device. Note: hard refresh wipes drafts because wizard state is session-only in the browser until Save & exit is used.

6Validation gates

Six checks stand between "selected" and "enrolled". Four are hard on both paths. Two soften for administrators. And unlike admin-led enrolment, there is no student override.

Gate What it checks Admin-led Self-enrol
Program rules Every subject must belong to the student's program structure. Failure returns 422. Hard Hard
Offering status Offering must be Active with Allow Enrolments = Yes. Hard Hard
Calendar constraints Today must sit inside every relevant date window — offering, period, self-enrol dates on the primary AP. Hard Hard
Duplicate prevention No re-enrolling in the same offering; no different offering of an already-completed or already-enrolled subject. Hard Hard
Requisite rules Pre, co, concurrent pre, anti, equivalent. Real CB evaluation per version, with AND/OR + score/grade/credit conditions. Overridable Hard
Standard load Total credit points vs the period's standard load. Admin: gate. Student: shown as an informational alert only. Overridable Info only
💡
Worth calling out to clients: validation is done by the same curriculum engine that powers admin-led enrolment — one source of truth for rules, whether staff or students drive the process.

· Duplicate-enrolment alerts

Two messages can appear on the review page: "Subject already completed" and "Subject already added — you've selected a different offering". Both are non-actionable — no +Add, no dialog. The student has to remove and re-pick.

7Confirm — what actually happens

The moment the student or admin clicks the button, five things happen server-side. Both journeys share the same transaction boundary, the same atomicity guarantee, and the same reporting hooks.

  1. Requisites re-evaluated

    Not trusted from the review page — requisites are checked again on the server, so a stale draft can't slip through.

  2. Program-rules pass repeated

    Ensures a subject wasn't removed from the program structure between selection and confirm.

  3. Enrolment records written

    One enrolment record per subject, plus academic-period enrolment records for any new periods.

  4. Financial liability created

    Tuition fees, plus the student services and amenities fee (SSAF) where SA-HELP applies.

  5. TCSI records prepared

    Reporting hooks fire so the enrolment appears in the next TCSI submission cycle.

🔒
Safe by design: confirms are protected against double-submission, and if any rule fails at the last moment (e.g. a requisite), the whole submission rolls back — a student can never end up half-enrolled.

8Adjust, drop, withdraw

Confirming isn't the end of the journey. Three ways to reverse or amend, each with its own window and its own owner.

a. Student-driven adjust

The adjust-scope wizard lets the student add or drop within an open window. Every drop is checked against the drop window. If the enrolment close date has passed but the drop deadline is still open, the wizard loads in drop-only mode.

Adjust your subjects→ Drop / add→ Review→ Subjects adjusted

b. Admin withdrawal

Withdrawal is admin-only, from the Manage Enrolment page. Beyond removing the enrolment, it handles the downstream cleanup:

💰

Financial liability

Fee refund or write-off, depending on timing relative to the census date.

📝

Outcome assignment

Withdrawn subjects get the correct outcome code (WD, WF, etc.) recorded against the enrolment.

🗂️

TCSI reporting

The withdrawal event is prepared for the next TCSI submission — no re-keying required.

🔐
What the student can't do. A student cannot withdraw themselves. Adjust-scope handles subject-level drops during the drop window; anything beyond that — leaving a program, cancelling an academic period — is admin territory.

Summary

The whole HE enrolment story, in one view — capabilities the platform delivers across both journeys.
